# 0012 — Outbound to people

**Status:** Accepted. Amended: see ADR 0011's *Amendment: a parseable agent file* (her description was not loaded
in the affected state). Subsystems: `Documentation/architecture/subsystems/role-skills.md`,
`Documentation/architecture/subsystems/specialist-roster.md`,
`Documentation/architecture/subsystems/orchestrator-charter.md` and
`Documentation/architecture/subsystems/plugin-packaging.md`.

## Decision

Nobody on the team asked whether text going out to people would offend someone, and nothing said who may send it.
The team had a member who asks a question of what ships (ADR 0011) and no rule for text that leaves the team as an
email, a report, a pull request or a comment. The rules are in `plugins/devhouse/team/protocol.md` (*Authority*,
*Voice*, *Release* and its *Outbound to people*, *Files*, *Contact*, *Tests*) and the charter's *Invariants*; this
ADR records why.

- **Remi, not a new member.** Her one question was "is it safe to publish?"; it becomes two, asked of any text
  that goes out to people: "is the data right for its reader?" and "how will it land?" A separate member for the
  second would be a second reviewer of the same text, and both questions are about what leaves the team.
- **The sending rule, in its conservative reading.** External text (outside the human's organisation) is drafted
  only, and so is internal text other than pull requests and PR or issue comments. A member posts one of those
  only on the human's approval for that act, in her own session, naming the repository, the target and the draft
  version; an edit needs a new approval; opening a new internal pull request may include the one push the
  approval names. The absolute reading was chosen because a loose "generally drafts" cannot be applied by a
  member at the moment of sending. The text ends with a fixed signature, `Generated by Claude <model>; posted with
  approval.`, with the session's own model filled in, and a fallback when the session cannot tell. Where a reader
  is unclear, the text is external. A project records its internal repositories in `CLAUDE.md` (`outbound
  repos:`); an unlisted repository is external.
- **The data check is relative to the reader.** For outbound text the finding is data this reader should not have;
  a report sent to the party it concerns legitimately holds that party's data. For a release or an audit-only
  run, the reader is the public, and her method's release list applies. The disclosure rule is unchanged and adds
  the risk: class, location and risk, never the value, and never another party's name.
- **No profiling.** "How will it land" needs a reader, and the temptation is to guess one. She uses only the
  reader facts the human gave (role and relationship), adds no attributes, infers nothing from a name or an email,
  and records reader facts only in the reader file, never in crew memory (ADR 0010's content rule).
- **Findings are advisory.** They go to the human beside the draft, she suggests a softer line only when asked,
  and an outbound finding never gates a merge or a unit. The human decides.
- **The route and the fallback.** Every outbound draft in the session form goes to the manager, who points Remi
  at it, and reaches the human only with her review beside it. When no review exists, the draft carries one of
  three exact lines (declined, absent, waived), distinct from the security waivers. A declined card holds for the
  rest of the session.
- **Cutting the unit path.** Checking a unit's deliverable at the unit, with a work-order mark, would have made a
  second route for the same text. It is cut: a unit's deliverable goes the way any outbound text goes, when the
  human asks for it to go out.
- **Auto-routing stops for her.** Her `description` carries no "use when" trigger and ends "Invoke only when
  named (@remi)", and the charter's roster introduction excepts her from description-based auto-dispatch. In the
  subagent form the orchestrator offers `@remi` and never auto-dispatches her. This also stops auto-routing for
  her release audits. That is the cost: she is dispatched only when named, so no task match sends her a draft
  nobody asked her to check.
- **Where the drafts live.** `<devhouse home>/outbound/<project>/`, outside the repository, versioned (`v<n>`),
  with the reader files, the summaries of conversations about a draft, and her reviews. The guard covers it; in
  the subagent form only the orchestrator writes there (apart from `@remi`'s release-notes drafts, ADR 0013). The
  folder is kept, and the human deletes it by hand.

## Consequences

- **The roster text changes.** Remi's Role text, `description`, method and second value change, and the charter
  and README rosters stay identical. No new member, so the counts do not change.
- **A new artefact kind.** `outbound` joins the kinds written outside `Documentation/`, with `validity` and
  `audit`.
- **The gap.** Nothing stops a member from sending text some other way; the rules are habits, as everywhere in the
  session form (ADR 0003). The release manager's review is only as good as the reader facts the human gave her.
- **The canonical statements are in one place each:** what a member may send in *Authority*; the scope, the
  questions, the route and the fallback in *Release* → *Outbound to people*; the files in *Files*; the guard in
  *Tests*; the subagent form in the charter.
